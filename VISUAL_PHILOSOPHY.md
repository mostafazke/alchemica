# Alchemica — Visual Philosophy

> This document is the **why**. For the **what**, see [copilot_CODE_BRIEF.md](copilot_CODE_BRIEF.md).

---

## The Room Has Moved Upstairs

For three weeks, Alchemica was a submarine control room. Metal panels, blinking indicators, cold and functional. Emotional temperature: 60°F. Air-conditioned. A game about Fire, Plasma, and Lava that somehow read as clinical data entry.

Then it became what it should have been: **an ancient workshop where serious work has been happening for a very long time.** You didn't build this room. You entered it. The brass shelf has been handled so many times the high points are worn bright and the recesses have deepened. The glass vessels have absorbed what they contained — the fire vessel is slightly amber from the inside, not from paint, from use. The stone surface holds heat from old reactions. Everything belongs here because everything has been here.

**The workshop is the same workshop. It has moved upstairs.**

A high window now. Morning light. The workbench is warm cream — aged parchment going golden where the sun crosses it. The same brass still catches. The same glass still holds what it contained. The same ancient materials in the same inhabited room, but now you can see the room properly. Not navigating by candlelight — working by day.

And the elements don't need darkness to glow. Fire is vivid against anything, because it's Fire. Water catches the light differently than stone. Earth absorbs it. Each element's own nature creates its visual presence — not contrast borrowed from a dark wall, but what it is against a warm neutral surface.

## The Five Emotions

### 1. Wonder — The Fog Is Promising

Reference: A well-stocked apothecary at morning. Every shelf goes further back than you can see. The near shelves are vivid in the window light; the far ones recede into amber shadow. Something is definitely happening in those shadows.

The shelf goes further back than you can see. Depth is inhabited, not empty. "I believe things are happening in those shadows I haven't discovered yet." The fire vessel glows from inside against the warm cream of the workbench — not lit from outside, illuminated by what it contains. Something you'd be slightly nervous to touch.

Wonder doesn't require darkness. It requires depth.

### 2. Discovery — Sun Through Stained Glass

Reference: Standing inside a cathedral on an overcast morning. The window is just muted parchment and dull lead. A cloud moves. The colors arrive — not appearing, *arriving* — pale cream blooming to vivid jewel tone in a single second. The same glass. Different light.

The new element was always there. You didn't create Steam — you released it from Fire and Water. The overlay begins at parchment — your workbench, neutral, waiting. Then it blooms: the color specific to this element's nature. Fire arrivals are deep amber-orange. Water arrivals bloom sapphire. Energy arrivals open into violet. The bloom is the revelation. Color is the name before the name.

Emergence, not delivery. The distinction is everything.

### 3. Mastery — The Kitchen at Year Six

Reference: A restaurant kitchen after six years. Hands go to the knife before eyes confirm it's there. The kitchen is fully lit. The mastery isn't navigating in the dark — it's moving without needing to think.

The beginner's shelf is a collection of equal strangers — a spreadsheet. The expert's shelf is a workshop lived in. Elements combined hundreds of times feel worn, like a well-used knife handle or a much-read binding. Rare elements still glow because they're genuinely new to *you*, not because a designer decided rare things glow.

The shelf becomes a portrait of the player, not a catalogue of the game.

### 4. Failure — The Dream That Retreats

Reference: Trying to recall a dream thirty minutes after waking. You feel the shape of it but the content retreats the harder you chase.

"No Reaction" is the door that locks. "Not yet" is the librarian returning an unavailable book — almost apologetically, not authoritatively. A single muted particle rises from the beaker against the warm surface. Rises, slows, desaturates on the way up, disappears. Communicates nothing specific. But it suggests the universe registered the attempt.

A door that is listening, not a door that is locked.

The failure moment should feel present enough to keep reaching. Unresolved enough to stay curious.

### 5. Power — The Thunderstorm Fifteen Miles Out

Reference: Standing on a beach, still sunny, sand still warm. But the light shifted to that yellow-green that exists nowhere else. Air pressure changed — felt in the chest before thought about. The water went completely flat. The birds stopped.

Power doesn't announce itself with noise — it announces itself by making everything around it quieter. Earth *sits*. Plasma doesn't sit — it *occupies*. A faint disturbance in its vicinity. Not animation — the shelf around it seems aware, the way water went flat before the storm. A pulse you're not sure you imagined. Like something breathing that shouldn't be breathing.

The player's finger should hesitate — not because the UI warns them, but because the light changed.

## What We Stole and From Whom

**Outer Wilds:** Material honesty. The ship doesn't look designed — it looks built by someone who needed it to work. Discovery = uncovering something that was always there. The ancient ruins predate the player and are indifferent to their presence. The ship log is the best discovery journal ever built into a game.

**Disco Elysium:** The principle that the interface is a physical object existing *in* the world, not layered on top of it. Every UI element feels used by a person before you. This holds as firmly in a daylit room as a dark one.

**Ghibli (Kiki's Delivery Service / Howl's Moving Castle):** The apothecary in the morning. Magic that lives in ordinary daylit spaces — a warm kitchen, an amber workshop window, a sunlit room full of things that do things. Power in small hands. Joy with weight.

**Obra Dinn:** Not the aesthetic — the discipline. Every visual element must answer: "What work are you doing that nothing else is doing?" If it can't answer, it doesn't belong in the room. Constraint creates presence.

**Little Alchemy 2:** The permission to be simple at the mechanical level. The precedent that casual alchemy works on a bright background. And still the ceiling — proof that simplicity alone produces something pleasant but forgettable the moment you close it. Alchemica aims for the space it proves is possible but doesn't inhabit.

**The gap Alchemica occupies:** Simple system + profound atmosphere. The formula the genre has never fully attempted.

## The Emotional Design Principles

1. **The room has received a window.** The workshop is warm, inhabited, ancient — and visible. Parchment cream under morning light. Not a clinical interface. An apothecary study that was here before you, and will be here after.

2. **Elements glow from their own nature.** Fire is vivid because it's Fire. Water is jewel-blue. Earth is verdant. Each element carries its own luminous identity against the warm neutral surface — not borrowing presence from a dark wall, earning it from what it is.

3. **Less is louder.** A single muted particle communicates more than a red error banner. A half-beat of silence creates more impact than a louder sound.

4. **The body knows before the mind.** The player's finger hesitates before touching Plasma — not because the UI said "dangerous" but because the light changed.

5. **Every moment earns its presence.** Nothing decorative. Every glow, every animation, every sound must answer: "What work are you doing that nothing else is doing?"

## Where We Are

The workshop moved upstairs.

Same ancient materials. Same worn brass, same inhabited shelf, same glass that absorbed what it contained. Morning light now comes through a high window and falls across aged parchment. The elements glow from their own nature against the warm surface of the workbench.

The room was never about the dark. It was always about the materials, the time, the evidence of serious work happening here for a very long time. All of that survives a window.

The shelf is still a portrait of the player. Discovery still feels like emergence, not delivery. Power still makes the room go quiet. Failure still listens.

That's not a redesign. That's a philosophy. And philosophies survive implementation — they guide every decision that the spec didn't anticipate, which is most of them.

The room is ready. Build Phase 1.
