### PHASE 1 — Alien Anthropologist: Diagnosis

**Mobile comfort note:** Colors are too dark — causes physical discomfort on mobile screens.

**What Alchemica accidentally communicates (current state):**
- A submarine control room repurposed as a lab. Metal panels, blinking indicators, cold and functional.
- Emotional temperature: 60°F. Air-conditioned. Not refreshing — just the building's default.
- Music genre: Ambient techno. Brian Eno on a server farm. Functional, intelligent, cold.
- The shelf: Eyes land on the teal selected-element border — the ONLY thing with temperature. Everything else is same weight, same darkness, equally forgettable.
- Successful reaction: "Operation successful. Log entry created." Clinical. A database write. Green confirmation. NOT a discovery.

**What Alchemica should communicate (desired direction):**
- Hogwarts meets a medieval alchemist's tower. Candlelight. Mystery. The smell of something burning. Discovery.
- Emotional temperature: 85°F with humidity. Warm, slightly dangerous. Something might boil over.
- Fire should feel warm when you touch it.
- Music: Early Hans Zimmer meets a Marrakech street market. Curious, layered, slightly dangerous. "You're about to discover something that changes everything."
- The shelf: Like a real apothecary. Eyes pulled by color, size, glow, rarity. The gold bottle stands out from the clay ones.
- Successful reaction: "You just did something that shouldn't be possible — and the universe noticed." Shock first, then joy. Screen feels briefly dangerous before rewarding.

**Core Visual Identity Crisis — Three Fault Lines:**
1. **Thermal void** — No warmth. A game about Fire, Plasma, Lava, Explosion reads like cold data.
2. **Flat hierarchy** — Every element has the same visual gravity. Rarity, energy, and category are invisible.
3. **Confirmation vs. revelation** — The reaction moment confirms instead of astonishes. The universe should *react*, not *log*.

### PHASE 2 — Emotion Orchestra

#### EMOTION 1 — WONDER

**Reference:** Spirited Away — Chihiro walks through the tunnel. Lanterns at dusk. Sky between amber and violet. Everything oversized. Food stalls glowing from within.

**Visual qualities of wonder (instinctive):**
- Light has a source you want to FIND — not a light that illuminates the scene but light that IS the scene. Lanterns don't light things, they ARE things. Objects glow from inside, not lit from outside.
- Depth you cannot fully see — background fades into mist/distance/darkness but feels INHABITED. "I believe things are happening in that darkness I haven't discovered yet." The fog is promising, not threatening.
- Scale slightly wrong — too tall, too long, makes you feel like a child in an adult world. "I don't know the rules here yet and I want to learn them."
- Things are slightly alive that shouldn't be — steam moves, lanterns flicker unevenly, nothing perfectly still. The world breathes at its own rhythm, indifferent to whether you're watching.

**Implication for Alchemica's wonder moment:**
- First screen = standing outside the tunnel. Warm light visible on the other side.
- The four starting elements are not the whole story — they're the entrance. The shelf should feel like it goes further back than you can see.
- Elements glow from within — luminous, not outlined, not highlighted. Like they contain something.
- Fire shouldn't be a red icon on a dark card. It should look like something you'd be slightly nervous to touch.

---

#### EMOTION 2 — DISCOVERY

**Reference:** Darkroom photography. Paper slides into the developer tray. Red light. Fifteen seconds of nothing — then the image arrives everywhere simultaneously, unevenly, like fog lifting. Violent and quiet at the same time.

**Visual qualities of the instant of creation (instinctive):**
- **Time thickens, doesn't stop.** Fifteen seconds feels longer than the hour before. Attention narrows to a single point. Everything outside that tray ceases to exist — not dramatically, politely.
- **Simultaneously small and enormous.** The tray is 8 inches wide. What's appearing feels infinite.
- **Completely silent.** Not quiet — silent. Sound comes after, when you exhale.
- **World contracts THEN explodes.** Everything collapses to the new thing appearing — then one beat later the mind rushes outward: what does this mean, what does this connect to.
- **Finding, not making.** Even though every decision led here — the element was always there and you revealed it. Discovery, not construction.
- **The arrival has resistance.** It doesn't appear cleanly. Chemistry is uneven, light was uneven, paper has texture. The image comes up *through* something. That resistance is what makes it feel real rather than generated.

**Implication for Alchemica's discovery moment:**
- The new element should not simply appear — it should DEVELOP. Arrive through something, like a photograph through chemistry. Not an instant pop. Not a clean fade-in. Something that takes ~1.2 seconds and feels like it's fighting to exist.
- World contracts at moment of reaction — everything except the beaker dims slightly. Not dramatically — just enough that peripheral vision registers "something is happening here."
- After the element arrives: the world expands. New card on the shelf pulses once, gently — like it just took its first breath.
- **Sound comes AFTER the visual.** A half-beat of silence at the exact moment of creation, then the sound. That half-beat is where wonder lives.
- Animation language should suggest the element was always latent — you didn't create Steam, you released it from Fire and Water. Show emergence, not delivery.

#### EMOTION 3 — MASTERY

**Reference:** A restaurant kitchen after six years. By year three, stopped reading the space. Hands go to the knife before eyes confirm it's there. The space becomes a language you think in, not translate.

**Visual qualities of earned familiarity (instinctive):**
- **The space stops performing.** A new space shows off — every object at full brightness, full presence, demanding to be catalogued. A mastered space relaxes. Things recede appropriately. The knife block doesn't announce itself. It simply is there when needed.
- **Eye moves differently.** Beginner's eye scans. Expert's eye *lands* — directly, without the scan. The visual implication: the path between intention and object becomes straight.
- **Peripheral vision activates.** Beginners use central vision for everything. Experts process the periphery. Mastered space communicates through peripheral signals, not direct announcement.

**The harder version — what the shelf looks like at day 30:**
- Beginner's shelf = a collection of equal strangers. Every card the same weight, same brightness, same distance from the eye. It looks like a spreadsheet.
- Expert's shelf = a workshop that's been lived in. Evidence of use. Elements combined hundreds of times feel worn in — not degraded, but familiar like a well-used tool handle.
- Rare/new elements still have fresh luminance — they haven't settled yet. They're still performing. Old ones have stopped performing because they don't need to.
- **Visual hierarchy earned, not assigned.** Rarity glows because it's genuinely new to *you*, not because a designer decided rare things glow. Day-1 elements feel like furniture. This-morning elements feel like guests.
- Discovery is vertical — everything rushes upward, expanding. Mastery is horizontal — already mapped, moving through it laterally, efficiently, without ceremony. Like a musician finding a note, not a tourist finding a landmark.

**Implication for Alchemica:**
- The shelf needs a **memory** — not just an unlock state but a *frequency state*. Elements used many times visually settle: slightly less border emphasis, more integrated into the shelf. Recently discovered elements still slightly luminous, unsettled, not yet absorbed into vocabulary.
- The expert's shelf should communicate at a glance the *shape of your own history*. You should be able to look at day-30 shelf and see — without reading anything — which are old friends and which are new arrivals.
- **The shelf becomes a portrait of the player, not a catalogue of the game.**

#### EMOTION 4 — FAILURE (the not-yet)

**Reference:** Trying to recall a dream thirty minutes after waking. You know it was there. You feel the shape of it — the emotional register, the approximate color. But the specific content retreats the harder you chase it. You keep reaching not because you expect to find it — because the reaching itself feels like it keeps the thing alive.

**Visual qualities of not-yet (instinctive):**
- **Texture without content.** Not looking at nothing — looking at something that hasn't resolved yet. Like static that almost makes a shape. Like hearing music through a wall — enough to know it exists, not enough to name it.
- **Very still.** Not violently still — genuinely quiet. The world doesn't shake or flash or protest. It simply stays exactly as it was. The absence of response IS the response.
- **A half-second of rechecking.** You reach, nothing happens, and for a half-second you reach again mentally. The mind refuses the null result briefly before accepting it.
- **Open question, not closed answer.** The dream isn't gone — it's withheld. There is a difference between "does not exist" and "not yet accessible." The feeling lives entirely in that difference.

**Implication for Alchemica's failure moment:**
- The beaker should NOT shake angrily. Should NOT flash red. Red = wrong. Wrong is not what's happening — *not yet* is what's happening.
- Elements should drift back gently. Not rejected — returned. The librarian returning an unavailable book, not the bouncer turning you away. Gesture is almost apologetic rather than authoritative.
- Chamber shows the ghost of what almost happened — a brief half-second of something beginning to form, a wisp of reaction that didn't find its footing, then dissolving. Not a full reaction that fails — the suggestion that something was listening, considered it, and isn't ready to show you yet.
- **Feedback text must never say "No Reaction."** That's the librarian stamping REJECTED. Say instead: *"Not yet." / "These aren't ready for each other." / "Something is missing." / "The combination is incomplete."* Language that implies the answer exists somewhere, not that the attempt was wrong.
- **The single particle:** One particle rises from the beaker when the reaction doesn't happen. Rises, slows, disappears. Does nothing useful. Communicates nothing specific. But it suggests something noticed the attempt. The universe registered it. A door that is listening, not a door that is locked.
- The failure moment should feel like the dream that retreats when you chase it. Present enough to keep reaching. Unresolved enough to stay curious.

#### EMOTION 5 — POWER

**Reference:** Standing on a beach watching a thunderstorm approach fifteen miles out over water. The beach still sunny. Sand still warm. But the light had shifted to that yellow-green that exists nowhere else in nature. Air pressure changed — felt in the chest before thought about. The water went completely flat and dark, like it was paying attention. The birds stopped. The storm hadn't arrived. But its presence preceded it by fifteen minutes.

**Visual qualities of genuine presence (instinctive):**
- **Everything else becomes more still.** Power doesn't announce itself with noise — it announces itself by making everything around it quieter. The birds stopped. The water flattened. Genuine power creates a stillness in its vicinity more arresting than any display would be.
- **Light changes quality, not quantity.** Not darker before the storm — differently lit. Same photons, but seemingly coming from the wrong direction, landing at the wrong angle. Power has its own light source, or it interferes with existing light. It doesn't add brightness — it displaces it.
- **Mass you feel without touching.** Standing next to a large ship engine — you don't hear it so much as feel it in your sternum. The air near genuine power has a different density. The body registers it before the eyes confirm it.
- **Completely patient.** The storm wasn't hurrying. Real power never performs urgency because it doesn't need to. It will still be powerful whether or not you're watching. That indifference is the most frightening quality.

**The harder version — Plasma and Lightning on the shelf:**
- Earth *sits*. Settled. Permanent. Comfortable with its own ordinariness.
- Plasma doesn't sit — it *occupies*. A faint disturbance in its immediate vicinity. Not animation — more like the shelf around it is slightly less stable. The other cards near it don't move, but they seem aware. The way water went flat before the storm.
- **Rarity glows warmly** — amber, gold, candlelight and age. **Power glows with a light that has no temperature.** Not cold, not warm — electromagnetic. The difference between a lantern and an arc welder. Both produce light. One you walk toward. One you don't look at directly.
- High-energy elements have a very faint, very slow pulse — not a designed animation pulse, but a subtle luminance shift that makes you uncertain whether you saw it or imagined it. Like something breathing that shouldn't be breathing. Like an engine running at a frequency just below hearing.
- **The player's finger should hesitate not because the UI warns them, but because something in the visual register says this one is different.** No red border. No warning icon. Just the quality of the light, the stillness of surrounding elements, and that half-imagined pulse that makes you look twice.
- The storm didn't need a warning sign. You knew.

### PHASE 3 — Sensory Exploration: The Material World

**The question:** What physical material is Alchemica made of?

**The answer:**

**The shelf:** Dark brass — not polished, not decorative. Working brass. Handled so many times the high points are worn bright and the recesses have gone almost black. Weight. Doesn't flex. Tapped with a fingernail it makes a sound that feels important. Faint scratches that aren't damage — they're history. This shelf has held things before you arrived and will hold things after.

**The vessels (element cards):** Glass — but not clean glass. Glass that has absorbed what it contained. The fire vessel is slightly amber from the inside — not from paint, from use. The water vessel has old apothecary glass quality — thickness uneven, light bends differently depending on where you look. You could see through them if the light was right. You can almost see through them now.

**The surface underneath:** Dark stone. Not cold marble. Something older. Stone that has been warm so long from proximity to furnaces and reactions that it holds heat even when nothing is burning. Doesn't look warm. Is warm.

**The critical reframe:**
- UI cards are not screens or data panels. They are **vessels sitting on a brass shelf on a warm stone workbench in a room that has been doing this work for a very long time.**
- The dark background isn't a design choice — it's the room.
- The ambient light isn't interface chrome — it's candlelight and light coming from the vessels themselves.

**The design test going forward:** Does this feel like it belongs in that room?

### PHASE 4 — Cross-Pollination: What to Steal

**1. Disco Elysium — Yes, but only the philosophy, not the surface.**
- Lives in the room in one way: every UI element feels used by a person before you.
- **STEAL:** The principle that the interface is a physical object existing in the world of the game — not a layer on top of it. The shelf labels should look like they were written by someone. Element descriptions should feel like marginalia in a working scientist's notebook, not a Wikipedia entry.
- **DON'T STEAL:** The decay, the sickly green — those belong to Disco Elysium's specific sadness.

**2. Outer Wilds — Yes. Completely and without reservation.**
- Closest existing thing to the room we built. Plywood and copper wire. Stone worn smooth by wind. The ship doesn't look designed — it looks built by someone who needed it to work.
- Discovery feeling = closest thing in games to the darkroom experience. Not receiving information — uncovering something that was always there. The ancient ruins predate the player entirely and are indifferent to their presence.
- **STEAL:** Material honesty. The sense the world existed before the player arrived. Knowledge accumulates as physical things — notes, recordings, locations — not database entries unlocking. The Outer Wilds ship log is the best discovery journal ever built into a game. The Alchemica discovery log should feel like that.

**3. Ghibli / Mononoke / Nausicaä — Yes for color temperature and danger language. No for everything else.**
- Specific thing Ghibli knows that almost no one else knows: **danger is the color of things in the process of burning, not the color assigned to danger.** The corrupted forest god doesn't turn red — it turns the color of oil, dark water, things decomposing into other things. The toxic jungle is beautiful before it's lethal.
- **STEAL:** Color logic — warm amber, deep violet, the specific green of things growing in darkness are more threatening than red ever is.
- **DON'T STEAL:** The softness, rounded edges, maternal warmth. Alchemica's room is older and drier. Less living forest, more ancient workshop.

**4. Obra Dinn — No, but one irreplaceable lesson.**
- Doesn't live in the room — too cold, too intellectual, too removed from the material warmth established. But it shares one wall.
- **STEAL:** The discipline, not the aesthetic. Every visual element should answer: *"What work are you doing that nothing else is doing?"* If it can't answer, it doesn't belong in the room. Constraint creates presence. Nothing decorative.

**5. Bōken no Sho / worn parchment Zelda — Yes, for one thing only.**
- Lives in the room in exactly one way: the document has been handled by someone who cared about what was in it. Worn edges, faded ink, slightly uneven text — communicate that information matters, has a history of being consulted and returned to.
- **STEAL:** The discovery log, element descriptions, player's notebook should feel not designed — *kept*. The difference between a receipt and a letter someone has read twenty times.
- **DON'T STEAL:** The parchment color, the medieval fantasy signaling. The room predates aesthetic periods.

**6. Little Alchemy / Doodle God — No. The cost of what they chose is the entire problem.**
- Don't live in the room. Flat, bright, completely surface-level. The cost: the player never believes in the world because there is no world, only a system. Pleasant and completely forgettable the moment you close it. Elements have no weight, no material quality, no history. Discovery feels like a correct quiz answer.
- **STEAL:** The permission to be simple at the mechanical level. The combination mechanic doesn't need to be more complex than theirs.
- **THE GAP:** Simple system, profound atmosphere. That is where Alchemica lives.

**Cross-Pollination Synthesis:**
> The room contains Outer Wilds' material honesty, Disco Elysium's principle that the interface is a physical object, Ghibli's color logic for danger and warmth, Obra Dinn's discipline of earned presence, and the Zelda logbook's feeling of something carefully kept. Little Alchemy shows exactly where the ceiling is if you choose surface over atmosphere — and exactly why Alchemica needs to choose differently.

### PHASE 5 — Six Thinking Hats

#### ⬜ WHITE HAT — Facts & Constraints

- Mobile OLED. Dark backgrounds can be extremely dark — no ambient lift. Interior-glow elements will punch hard. Verify readability at minimum brightness.
- Ages 10+. Some deuteranopia/protanopia. Category identity cannot rely on hue alone — must encode in luminance, texture, or shape also.
- 44×56px minimum touch targets. Visual complexity (glow, texture, pulse) must not reduce perceived tap area.
- WCAG AA: 4.5:1 normal text, 3:1 large text. Warm amber glow against warm dark backgrounds is in the contrast-danger zone — needs careful calibration.
- Every color currently hardcoded hex. No CSS custom properties or tokens. Redesign = full system replacement.
- **Base color decision:** Warm dark stone. Not near-black navy. Something like `#0e0a07` rather than `#0a0e1a`. The shift is subtle in hex values and enormous in emotional temperature. Every element glow reads differently against warm dark than cool dark. Amber, brass, apothecary glass — they fight a cool navy background. Against warm stone they emerge from it. Contrast is a solvable engineering problem within the warm dark direction. Don't abandon the direction to solve the problem.

#### 🔴 RED HAT — Emotion & Instinct (complete)

**What the design wants to be:**

1. **Warm in the dark.** Not lighter — warmer. The distinction matters enormously. A lantern in daylight is an object. A lantern at night is a world. Keep the dark. Change its temperature from navy-cold to stone-warm.

2. **Ancient without being archaic.** The room predates periods. It doesn't perform oldness. It simply is old the way stone is old. Not steampunk. Not medieval fantasy. Not a costume.

3. **Quiet.** Every principle built across five emotions pointed toward restraint — half-beat of silence, power making surroundings quieter, the librarian not the bouncer, the single rising particle. Alchemica should feel like a place where you instinctively lower your voice. Not somber — *concentrated*. The feeling of a room where serious work is happening and you don't want to disturb it. Little Alchemy is cheerful and noisy. This is not that.

4. **Trusts the player.** Show less. Mean more. The player who notices the amber tint deepening in the fire vessel after fifty uses should feel like they discovered something about the game itself — a secret layer of craft most players will feel without ever articulating. The current UI announces everything, confirms everything, labels everything twice. The new visual language assumes a perceptive player.

5. **The only game that makes you feel like a discoverer, not a player completing a task.**
